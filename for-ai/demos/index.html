sleep

Question: How can I make enough room for sleep without turning my schedule into a rigid optimization experiment?

Route: brali:topic:sleep-circadian

Trusted recommendations

  • Protect Enough Sleep Opportunity and Track Daytime Function brali:protocol:ideal-sleep-hours-finder · reviewed
    Choose a bedtime-to-wake window that fits your obligations and gives you a reasonable opportunity for enough sleep. For a week or two, record only a few things: roughly when you tried to sleep, when you got up, whether the night was unusually disrupted, and how alert or sleepy you felt during the day. Look for repeated patterns, not single-night scores. Change one practical constraint at a time, such as moving bedtime earlier when your current schedule routinely leaves too little time for sleep.
    Reviewed source

Evidence boundaries

  • brali:evidence-decision:sleep-opportunity-extension-2021 · propose-protocol
    Behavioral interventions designed to extend sleep increased sleep duration on average compared with control or baseline. Direct interventions that specified a sleep schedule tended to have larger effects, but results were highly heterogeneous.
    Reviewed source

memory

Question: How can I use active recall to remember what I study, and what are its evidence boundaries?

Route: brali:topic:memory

Trusted recommendations

  • Separate Review Sessions Instead of Cramming brali:protocol:spaced-recall-coach · reviewed
    Choose one set of verbal material and decide how long you need to retain it. Split the planned repetitions across at least two sessions separated by time. Use a longer gap when the required retention period is longer, then check final recall and adjust the next schedule instead of treating one interval as universally optimal.
    Reviewed source
  • Use Active Recall for Retention, Then Practice Application brali:protocol:active-recall-test-yourself · reviewed
    Choose a small set of material you want to retain. Put the source out of view and answer questions or explain the material from memory. Then compare your response with the source, correct mistakes, and note what you could not retrieve. If success ultimately means solving, writing, speaking, calculating, or performing, add a separate task that practices that application.
    Reviewed source
  • Try Quiet Wakeful Rest After Learning brali:protocol:avoid-list-interference-memory-retention · reviewed
    When you finish a focused learning episode, put aside the material and spend a brief period awake in a quiet, low-stimulation setting. Avoid immediately switching to social media, problem solving, reading new material, or another cognitively demanding task. Choose a duration that is practical for you, then return to normal activity.
    Reviewed source

Evidence boundaries

  • brali:evidence-decision:distributed-practice-verbal-recall-boundary-2006 · support-existing
    For verbal material measured by later recall, separating repeated study episodes by a meaningful interval generally supports better retention than concentrating the same material into massed study. The spacing associated with the best later recall tended to increase as the intended retention interval increased. For material that must be retained over months or years, the reviewed evidence supports distributing study across days or longer rather than completing all review in one sitting or one day. These findings justify the rewritten Brali action only within a verbal-recall boundary and without one universal schedule.
    Reviewed source
  • brali:evidence-decision:retrieval-procedural-application-2026 · challenge-existing
    Adding retrieval practice to worked examples improved long-term retention of the spelling rules in this classroom study, but the combined group did not outperform worked examples alone on correct rule application after one week.
    Reviewed source
  • brali:evidence-decision:testing-effect-direct-forward-2026 · challenge-existing
    Retrieval practice can improve memory, but the literature commonly labeled as the testing effect mixes at least two distinct effects. In this meta-analysis, 66% of included testing effects came from mixed studies and 34% from direct studies, with mixed studies producing larger effects.
    Reviewed source
  • brali:evidence-decision:testing-versus-restudy-retention-boundary-2014 · support-existing
    Across the included testing-versus-restudy literature, retrieval testing produced better later retention on average than equivalent-duration restudy. The pooled random-effects estimate was positive, but effects varied substantially across studies and testing conditions. This supports Brali's bounded recommendation to retrieve information from memory, check the answer, and use that loop when retention is the target.
    Reviewed source
  • brali:evidence-decision:wakeful-rest-memory-2026 · propose-protocol
    A short period of quiet, minimally interfering wakeful rest after learning can reduce forgetting on average. The pooled effect is substantially smaller and less consistent in healthy young to middle-aged adults than in older adults or patient samples.
    Reviewed source

task-initiation

Question: I am procrastinating on a difficult task. What is a small way to get started?

Route: brali:topic:task-initiation

Trusted recommendations

  • Use a Short Phrase to Choose the Next Step brali:protocol:ad-astra-per-aspera-motivation-tracker · practical
    When you feel stuck, pause and repeat a short phrase once. Name what is difficult in plain language, then choose one action that is small enough to begin now. Skip the phrase if it feels hollow, pressuring or unrelated to the real problem.
