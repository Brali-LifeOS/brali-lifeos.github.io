Plan Your Workday in Three Simple Blocks

Work Smart with the 3-3-3 Routine

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Divide the workday into up to three broad blocks. Give each block one to three clear outcomes that fit the time you actually have. Replan when meetings, urgent work, or delays change the day.

Check-in: Did the blocks help me choose what mattered, and was any block overloaded, too vague, or too rigid?

A three-block plan is a simple way to give a workday some shape without scheduling every minute. Divide the day into a few broad periods, choose a small number of outcomes for each, and revise the plan when reality changes.

1. Start with the day you actually have

Look at fixed meetings, deadlines, breaks, and other commitments first. Build work blocks around those constraints instead of designing an ideal day that disappears at the first calendar invitation.

2. Create a few broad blocks

Use three blocks if that fits naturally: for example, early work, middle of day, and later work. Different hours or only two blocks are equally valid. The purpose is simply to reduce one large day into smaller planning windows.

3. Choose one to three outcomes per block

Write what should be different by the end of the block. Prefer a small number of clear outcomes over nine mandatory tasks. If one task is large enough to fill the block, let it.

4. Leave room for change

Do not plan every minute. If your work is interruption-heavy, leave visible space for reactive work. When something urgent arrives, decide what moves rather than quietly adding more work to the same block.

5. Replan between blocks

At a natural transition, take a brief look at what finished and what changed. Carry forward only work that still matters. A plan is useful when it can be updated cheaply.

6. Review the fit

At the end of the day, notice whether your blocks were realistic. If they were repeatedly overloaded, reduce the number of outcomes next time rather than making the rules stricter.

Questions to consider

Do I need exactly three blocks and three tasks in each?

No. Three-by-three is a planning template, not a productivity law. Use fewer blocks or fewer outcomes when the day is short, meeting-heavy, or unpredictable.

What should go inside a block?

Use outcomes you can recognize as done, such as draft the first section, review the data, or answer the three important requests. Avoid filling a block with a long list of tiny tasks.

What if the plan changes during the day?

Replan. Move, reduce, or drop an outcome when new work changes the available time. The structure should help you choose, not punish you for having a real workday.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
