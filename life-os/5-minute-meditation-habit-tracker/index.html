Try a Short Breath Meditation

Mini-Meditation Sessions

Protocol summary · reviewed

Try this

Set aside a short period somewhere comfortable and safe. Let your breathing stay natural, notice the sensations of breathing, and when your mind wanders gently bring attention back. Five minutes can be a convenient starting point, but use a duration and routine that work for you rather than treating twice-daily five-minute sessions as a required dose.

Check-in: Did I practise, how did focusing on the breath feel, and was returning attention useful, neutral or uncomfortable for me today?

Meditation can be as simple as setting aside a little time, sitting comfortably and returning attention to the breath when the mind wanders. NHS guidance emphasises that a wandering mind is normal and that there is no single correct session length. Brali therefore uses a short sit as a personal experiment, not a prescribed five-minute treatment or a test of whether your heart rate changes.

1. Choose a comfortable, safe place

Sit somewhere you can pause without needing to monitor traffic, machinery or another safety-critical task. You do not need special equipment or a special posture; choose a position that is comfortable and reasonably alert.

2. Let the breath be natural

Notice the sensations of breathing without forcing a counting pattern, long exhale or breath hold. If a simple word such as ‘in’ and ‘out’ helps you stay with the breath, use it lightly rather than trying to control the physiology.

3. Notice when the mind wanders

Thoughts, sounds and plans will pull attention away. When you notice that happening, gently bring attention back to the breath. NHS guidance explicitly describes mind wandering as a normal part of learning meditation.

4. Treat five minutes as a container, not a dose

If five minutes fits your day, use it as a convenient timer. A shorter or longer sit can also be reasonable. There is no need to complete two sessions a day or to chase a fixed number of minutes for the practice to count.

5. Notice if this practice is not helping

Mindfulness is not right for everyone. If focusing inward feels uncomfortable or makes you feel worse, stop, shorten the practice, use an external anchor such as sounds, or choose a different wellbeing activity. Do not force the session to protect a streak.

6. Review the experience

In Brali, record whether you practised and what the experience was like. Avoid turning pulse, HRV, number of thoughts or a calmness score into proof that meditation worked. After several attempts, decide whether this is a useful practice for you and what duration or context makes sense.

Questions to consider

Does meditation need to be five minutes twice a day?

No. Five minutes can make the practice easy to start, but NHS guidance says there is no rule about how long a meditation session should last and encourages people to find a routine that works for them. Brali does not treat two five-minute sessions as a therapeutic dose.

What if my mind keeps wandering?

Mind wandering is a normal part of meditation. Notice that attention moved and gently bring it back to the breath. The return is part of the practice, not evidence that you are doing it badly.

What if mindfulness makes me feel worse?

Stop or choose another wellbeing practice. NHS guidance notes that mindfulness is not right for everyone and some people find it unhelpful or feel worse. If significant anxiety, low mood or another mental-health difficulty is affecting daily life, use appropriate professional support.
