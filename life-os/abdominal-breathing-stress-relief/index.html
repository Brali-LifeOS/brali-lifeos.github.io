Comfortable Diaphragmatic Breathing

Use slow, comfortable breathing as a relaxation exercise

Protocol summary · reviewed

Try this

Sit or lie in a comfortable position, let the breath slow without forcing a count, notice the abdomen rise and fall, and stop after a few easy cycles or whenever you prefer.

Check-in: Did the exercise feel comfortable and leave you ready for the next ordinary action? Stop if you feel dizzy, short of breath, distressed, or otherwise unwell.

Evidence: Reviewed · source · NCCIH describes slow, deep or diaphragmatic breathing as a relaxation technique and notes that evidence for specific health conditions is limited; relaxation practices should not replace conventional care.

Review note: Reviewed against current NCCIH relaxation guidance. Public copy is limited to comfortable diaphragmatic breathing as a general relaxation exercise, removes fixed-rate and treatment claims, and explicitly says not to replace conventional care.

Safety: This is general educational material, not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, or a substitute for professional care.

Diaphragmatic breathing is one form of relaxation practice. Brali keeps the exercise deliberately simple: comfortable posture, unforced breathing, brief attention to abdominal movement, and no promise that one breathing pattern treats a health condition.

1. Get comfortable

Sit or lie in a position where breathing feels easy. Relax unnecessary tension in the shoulders and jaw.

2. Notice the abdomen move

Let the breath become slower if that happens comfortably. Notice the abdomen rise on the inhale and fall on the exhale without trying to maximize depth.

3. Avoid forcing a count

You do not need to reach a particular breaths-per-minute target or hold your breath. Comfort is a better boundary for a general-purpose relaxation exercise.

4. Keep the care boundary

Relaxation techniques can be used alongside ordinary self-care, but they are not a substitute for medical or mental-health care. Stop the exercise if it feels unwell or unsafe.

Questions to consider

Do I need a fixed breathing ratio or target rate?

No. This protocol deliberately avoids a required count. Use a pace that feels comfortable and unforced.

Is this a treatment for anxiety, blood pressure, or another condition?

No. Brali presents it as a general relaxation exercise. It is not a diagnosis or treatment and should not replace care recommended by a health professional.

When should I stop?

Stop if the exercise causes dizziness, shortness of breath, distress, pain, or other concerning symptoms. Return to normal breathing and seek appropriate care when needed.
