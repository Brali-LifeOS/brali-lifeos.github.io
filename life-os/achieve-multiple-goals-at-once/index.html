One Move, Several Benefits

Prefer actions that serve more than one priority without hiding trade-offs

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Name two priorities, brainstorm one concrete action that can serve both, and reject the combination if it makes either priority weaker or unclear.

Check-in: Did combining the priorities simplify the plan, or did it create a compromised task that serves neither one well?

Some priorities can share an action. The useful trick is not to chase maximum efficiency, but to notice compatible moves while keeping trade-offs visible.

1. Name two real priorities

Choose priorities that are already important. Do not invent a second goal just to make the technique fit.

2. Find one concrete action

Ask whether a single observable action can serve both priorities. Keep the action specific enough that you know when it is done.

3. Check the trade-offs

Notice what gets worse when the goals are combined. If quality, attention, privacy, or safety drops, separate the actions.

4. Review the result

After trying it, decide whether the combination simplified your plan or merely made it more complicated.

Questions to consider

Should I always combine goals?

No. Combine them only when one clear action genuinely serves both. Separate goals when the combination creates distraction or weakens the result.

What is a simple example?

A walking meeting can combine movement and a low-complexity conversation, but it would be a poor fit for a discussion that requires detailed documents or privacy.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
