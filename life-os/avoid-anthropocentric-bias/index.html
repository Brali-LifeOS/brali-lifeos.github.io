Separate Observation from Human-Like Interpretation

Check Anthropocentric Thinking

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Choose one behavior or output. Record only what you could point to or reproduce. Write the human-like interpretation you first considered, then add a system-specific alternative and one low-risk way to check between them. Keep uncertainty visible when you cannot check.

Check-in: What did I directly observe, what interpretation did I add, and what alternative or check changed my next action?

Review note: The inherited article contained unsupported survey ranges, volunteer outcomes, immediate-effect claims and rigid observation targets. The effective public record is rewritten as a low-risk observation-versus-interpretation check without an evidence claim.

Human-like language is convenient, but it can hide the difference between what happened and the motive we imagined. This protocol keeps the first interpretation visible while adding a description and alternative that fit the animal, plant, machine or model being considered.

1. Name the system and the question

State what you are trying to understand: an animal's behavior, a plant's condition, a machine's response or a model's output. Keep the question narrow enough to inspect.

2. Record observable facts

Write what moved, changed, appeared, repeated or stopped. Avoid motive words in this first pass. A fact should be something another observer could check or an operator could reproduce.

3. Surface the human-like interpretation

Write the first label that came to mind, such as stubborn, jealous, confused or trying to help. Treat it as an interpretation rather than a property you have established.

4. Add a system-specific alternative

Consider environment, learned behavior, physical condition, rules, inputs, sensors, data or constraints that could fit the same observation. Prefer an explanation that points to something checkable.

5. Choose a safe check or preserve uncertainty

Change one reversible condition, inspect a log, gather another observation or ask a qualified person. When no safe check is available, keep both explanations open instead of inventing certainty.

Questions to consider

Does this mean animals never have emotions?

No. The protocol asks you to separate observation from interpretation, not to deny animal experience. Welfare or health concerns should be handled with appropriate expertise rather than confident guessing.

Can I still use human metaphors for machines or models?

Yes, when the metaphor helps communication and its limit is explicit. Do not let words such as wants, knows or refuses replace a description of the actual rule, input or output.

What if I cannot test the alternative explanation?

Label it as an alternative rather than a conclusion. Choose a reversible action and record what information would change your view.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
