Check Whether a Streak Changes the Next Event

Trust the Odds

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Describe the next event and the recent streak. Ask what carries information or state from earlier outcomes into the next one. If there is no connection, ignore the streak and use the same probability or decision rule. If a connection may exist, identify the relevant state and gather that information. Use a pre-defined stop rule for repeated high-cost decisions.

Check-in: What connection did I identify between past and next events, and did my action follow the rule or merely the feeling that an outcome was due?

A run of similar outcomes can make the opposite result feel due. Before acting on that feeling, check whether the process carries any information from earlier outcomes into the next event.

1. State the next event and the streak

Write the event you are deciding about and the sequence that is influencing you. This separates the upcoming event from the story attached to the recent run.

2. Look for a real connection

Ask what carries state from one event to the next: changing inventory, wear, shared conditions, learning, policy, season or another observable factor. A streak by itself is not that connection.

3. Use the same rule when events are independent

When earlier outcomes do not alter the next event, keep the same probability, stake or decision rule. Do not increase commitment because an outcome feels overdue.

4. Inspect the relevant state when events are connected

When a connection may exist, gather information about that state rather than using the streak as a substitute. Update the decision only from information relevant to the connection.

5. Use a pre-defined stop rule

For repeated high-cost choices, decide in advance when to stop, pause or seek another view. Do not rewrite the rule after each outcome merely to keep the sequence going.

Questions to consider

Does every streak come from independent events?

No. Some processes carry state through changing conditions, depletion, learning, wear or shared inputs. The task is to identify a real connection rather than assume either independence or dependence from the streak alone.

What should I do when the connection is unknown?

Pause the high-cost action, gather relevant information and use a conservative rule. Unknown dependence is not a reason to increase a bet or commitment.

Is this gambling or financial advice?

No. The protocol does not recommend gambling, trading or continuing after losses. A pre-defined stop rule and choosing not to participate can be the responsible decision.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
