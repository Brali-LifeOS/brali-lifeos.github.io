Turn a Large Goal into the Next Verifiable Step

Make the finish condition and dependencies visible.

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Write what done looks like, list the major deliverables and external dependencies, split uncertain work until each next step has an observable output, choose the smallest useful step, complete it, and update the map from what you learned.

Check-in: What became complete or clearer, what dependency blocks the next step, and what is the smallest observable deliverable now?

Evidence: Practical guidance; no evidence-like claim is detected in the current source record.

Review note: The inherited goal-decomposition article contained unsupported completion-rate claims, fabricated experiments, arbitrary duration rules, and broad motivational promises. The effective public record is rewritten as a concrete decomposition protocol based on finish conditions, dependencies, observable deliverables, and iterative replanning.

Large goals often hide unclear finish conditions, dependencies, unknowns, and tasks that are still too broad to begin. Decomposition makes those boundaries visible without pretending the first plan will remain correct.

1. Define what done means

Write one observable finish condition. Replace labels such as improve the site with a result someone could inspect, check, approve, publish, or use.

2. Map deliverables and dependencies

List the few outputs required for the finish condition and mark what depends on another person, decision, system, or unknown.

3. Choose the next verifiable step

Break broad work into a deliverable or learning step with a visible result. Complete it, then revise the map and choose the next useful deliverable.

Questions to consider

Should every future step be planned before starting?

No. Map enough to see the finish condition, major dependencies, and next useful action. Update the decomposition as new information changes the plan.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
