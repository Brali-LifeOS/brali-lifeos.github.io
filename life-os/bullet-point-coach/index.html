Use a Short Bullet Outline Before You Speak

Prepare cue points for the purpose, key information and requested next step.

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Write the purpose of the conversation, the key facts or updates the listener needs, the decision or request if there is one, and any important risk or unknown. Put the cues in a useful order, remove duplicate points, then speak from them and adjust when the conversation changes.

Check-in: Did the outline make the purpose, key information and next step easy to find, and which cue should be changed before the next use?

A speaking outline is useful when you need structure without reading a script. The goal is to preserve the route through the conversation: why you are speaking, what the listener needs to know, where uncertainty sits, and what should happen next.

1. Write the purpose first

State what this interaction needs to accomplish: inform, decide, ask, align, escalate, explain, or collect feedback. A visible purpose makes it easier to remove details that are interesting but not useful for this conversation.

2. Add the minimum useful cues

List the facts, changes, constraints, risks, or examples the listener needs. Use wording you can understand at a glance. Keep exact figures, names, or commitments only when they are verified and necessary.

3. Make the ask or next step explicit

If you need a decision, question, owner, approval, or follow-up, give it its own cue. Do not hide the requested action inside background information.

4. Speak from the outline, not at it

Use the bullets as navigation. Expand only where the listener needs detail, skip a cue when the point is already resolved, and change direction when new information matters. Afterward, update the outline if a missing or confusing cue caused friction.

Questions to consider

How many bullets should I use?

Use as many as are needed to preserve the structure and no more. A short update may need only a few cues; a complex presentation may need grouped sections. The useful constraint is readability at a glance, not a universal number.

Should the bullets be full sentences?

Only when a sentence must be stated precisely. Otherwise use short cues that remind you of the point while allowing natural speech. If the outline becomes a page of paragraphs, it is functioning as a script rather than a speaking aid.

What belongs in a decision-oriented update?

Make the current state, the important change or constraint, and the requested decision or next step easy to locate. Add supporting detail only when the listener needs it to make the decision.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
