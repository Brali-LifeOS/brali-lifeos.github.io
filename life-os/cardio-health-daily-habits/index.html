Build Toward a Weekly Movement Baseline

Turn general activity guidance into a realistic weekly plan

Protocol summary · reviewed

Try this

Look at the coming week, mark the activity you already do, then add realistic walking, moderate or vigorous activity, and strength sessions where they fit. Build gradually rather than treating the guideline as a one-day target.

Check-in: Does the plan fit your current ability, schedule and any guidance from your health professional? Adjust it rather than forcing a target that is inappropriate for you.

Evidence: Reviewed · source · The American Heart Association recommends that adults aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity per week, or a combination, and include muscle-strengthening activity.

Review note: Reviewed against American Heart Association physical-activity guidance. Public copy is narrowed to planning movement toward the adult weekly activity baseline, with explicit adaptation to ability and professional medical advice.

Safety: This is general educational material, not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, or a substitute for professional care.

Heart-health guidance is much more useful when it becomes a weekly plan instead of a pile of heroic intentions. This protocol uses the American Heart Association activity baseline as orientation while keeping the actual plan adaptable to the person.

1. Mark what you already do

Start with the walking, active commuting, exercise, sport or strength work already present in a normal week. Do not erase existing movement just because it is informal.

2. Find realistic activity slots

Choose several places where additional movement can fit without requiring a complete lifestyle redesign.

3. Build toward the weekly baseline

The AHA baseline for adults is at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity per week, or a combination. Add toward it gradually when appropriate rather than treating it as an all-or-nothing threshold.

4. Adapt to your situation

Health conditions, symptoms, pregnancy, disability, medications and current fitness can change what is appropriate. Follow individualized medical advice when you have it.

Questions to consider

Do I need to complete 150 minutes immediately?

No. The weekly recommendation is a direction, not a demand to jump from your current level to the full target at once. Build activity in a way that fits your abilities and circumstances.

What counts as moderate activity?

The AHA includes examples such as walking, dancing and gardening. The appropriate intensity depends on the person and activity.

What if I have symptoms or a medical condition?

Use guidance from your health professional when it applies. This Brali page is general educational material, not an individualized exercise prescription.
