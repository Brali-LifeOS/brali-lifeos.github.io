Separate Observed Facts From Assumed Intent

Check what happened before deciding why another person did it.

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Write the observable event without motive language, mark the inferred intent as an interpretation, list other explanations that fit the known facts, check the information that is reasonably available, and ask a neutral clarifying question when another person can resolve the ambiguity.

Check-in: What did I directly observe, what intent did I infer, what else could explain it, and what information would actually distinguish those explanations?

The effective public record separates observable facts from inferred intent and uses alternative explanations, relevant checks and neutral clarification for ordinary ambiguous situations.

An event and its motive are different kinds of information. A missed message, changed task, abrupt reply, failed system or unexpected decision can be observable; the conclusion that someone intended a particular consequence may still be an interpretation. This protocol keeps those layers separate long enough to choose the next useful check.

1. State the event without motive language

Write only what could be checked by another person: what changed, what was said, what record exists, or what action occurred. Remove words that already imply a motive unless that motive was explicitly stated.

2. Mark the inferred intent as an interpretation

Name the explanation currently in your mind, then label it as an inference rather than a confirmed fact. This does not make the explanation wrong; it makes clear which part still needs support.

3. Check plausible alternatives and available information

Consider other explanations that fit the same event, such as error, missing context, conflicting priorities, a technical failure, or a deliberate choice with a different purpose. Inspect relevant records or context when doing so is appropriate and proportionate.

4. Clarify before converting inference into accusation

When another person can resolve the ambiguity, ask about the event and missing context directly. If the situation belongs to a formal safety, security, compliance or legal process, follow that process rather than improvising an informal investigation.

Questions to consider

Does this mean I should assume good intent?

No. The protocol asks you to avoid treating an unverified motive as a fact. A benign explanation and a deliberate explanation can both remain possible until the available information separates them.

What if the situation involves a real safety, security or legal concern?

Use the appropriate policy, incident process or qualified support for that domain. Do not delay required protective action merely to complete this reflection exercise, and do not use this protocol as a substitute for formal investigation.

What is a neutral clarification?

Describe the event you can verify and ask for the missing context without embedding the accusation in the question. For example, ask what changed or who made a decision before asserting a motive you cannot yet establish.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
