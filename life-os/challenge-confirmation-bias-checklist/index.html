Run a Disconfirming-Evidence Check

Test one claim against evidence that could weaken it

This checklist is for a concrete information problem: you have a claim or interpretation and want to test whether the evidence you are considering could actually change it. Brali owns the workflow. Cognitive Biases owns the broader mechanism, evidence, and diagnostic limits.

1. State the claim precisely

Write the claim in a form that could be wrong. Replace broad labels with an observable statement, forecast, or interpretation tied to the decision.

2. Define what would weaken it

Before searching, name at least one result, observation, or credible source that would make you less confident. This gives the search a real test instead of a vague instruction to be open-minded.

3. Inspect relevant evidence

Look deliberately for evidence that bears on the test. Prefer sources appropriate to the claim. Disagreement alone does not make a source stronger, and agreement alone does not make it weaker.

4. Update the decision, not just the notes

Record what you found and whether it changes confidence, the next action, or the need for more information. The success condition is a fair test, not a forced reversal.

Questions to consider

Do I need to find an opposing opinion?

No. The useful target is evidence that could change the claim, not disagreement for its own sake. A contrary opinion without relevant evidence may add noise rather than information.

What counts as a useful source?

Match the source to the claim. Primary data, original research, official documentation, or a well-scoped review may be useful depending on the question. Source quality does not come from whether it agrees with you.
