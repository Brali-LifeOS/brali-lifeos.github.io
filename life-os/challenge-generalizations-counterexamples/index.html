Counterexample Check

Test absolute statements against real exceptions

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Write the absolute statement, underline the universal word, and find one genuine case where the statement does not hold. Then rewrite the claim more precisely.

Check-in: Did the counterexample change the claim, or was the original statement already precise enough to survive the check?

Evidence: Practical guidance; no evidence-like claim is detected in the current source record.

Review note: Public content is replaced by a generic counterexample logic check. The legacy NLP collection is retained only as compatibility navigation; the protocol makes no claim that NLP is scientifically validated or necessary for the technique.

Absolute language can hide useful detail. A counterexample check forces a broad statement to meet at least one real exception before you use it as a rule.

1. Capture the exact claim

Write the sentence as it appears in your head or conversation. Preserve words such as always, never, everyone, nobody, impossible, or guaranteed.

2. Find a genuine exception

Look for one real case that does not fit. Avoid inventing a hypothetical exception solely to win an argument.

3. Rewrite more precisely

Replace the absolute statement with a narrower description that reflects what you actually know.

4. Choose the action from the narrower claim

Make the next decision using the more precise version rather than the emotionally stronger one.

Questions to consider

Is this an NLP technique?

The page lives in a legacy NLP collection, but the protocol itself is simply a logic check. It makes no claim about NLP as a method.

What if I cannot find a counterexample?

Keep the claim provisional and check whether it is narrow enough to be tested. Failure to find an exception quickly does not automatically make a broad statement true.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
