Trace Repeated Claims Back to Their Underlying Sources

Check whether several pages are independent or are repeating the same origin.

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Write the claim, list the pages or reports repeating it, follow their citations or links toward the origin, note shared documents, datasets, organizations or copied wording, and classify the set as independent origins, shared origin, mixed, or unclear.

Check-in: Which items looked separate, what underlying origin did each one use, where did they overlap, and how many genuinely distinct origins remain?

Evidence: Practical guidance; no evidence-like claim is detected in the current source record.

Review note: The inherited article contained unsupported claims about repetition and judgment, fabricated training outcomes, rigid timing targets and high-stakes examples. The effective public record is narrowed to tracing repeated claims back to their underlying documents, datasets or organizations so duplicated summaries are not mistaken for independent support.

Seeing the same statement in several places can create the appearance of several independent confirmations even when the pages trace back to one origin. Source lineage separates repetition from independence: follow the chain, record shared origins, and count distinct underlying material rather than distinct browser tabs.

1. Freeze the repeated claim

Write the specific statement that appears across the items. Keep wording close enough that you can tell whether the pages are truly making the same claim or only discussing the same topic.

2. Trace each item toward its origin

Follow citations, links, named documents, announcements, datasets and attributed quotations. Stop when you can identify the material that the item actually depends on, or when the chain becomes unavailable.

3. Mark shared lineage

Record when multiple items rely on the same underlying document, dataset, organization, author group, announcement or copied wording. Also note mixed cases where the items share one origin but add genuinely separate material.

4. Classify independence explicitly

Label the set as independent origins, shared origin, mixed, or unclear. Use that classification when summarizing what you found so repeated summaries do not silently become additional independent support.

Questions to consider

Do different websites count as independent sources?

Not automatically. Two websites can repeat the same announcement, document, dataset or earlier article. Follow the attribution chain far enough to identify what each page actually relies on.

What if I cannot reach the original material?

Mark the lineage as unclear. You can still record which intermediate sources are shared, but do not turn an unresolved chain into a claim of independence.

Does a shared source make the claim wrong?

No. This protocol only prevents repeated publication from being counted as multiple independent origins. Whether the underlying claim is correct requires a separate evaluation of the material itself.
