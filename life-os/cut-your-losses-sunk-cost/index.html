Judge a Commitment by What Comes Next

A quick stop, continue, or change check

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Name the decision. Separate unrecoverable past cost from future cost. List the realistic benefit of continuing, the best alternative use of the same resources, and any obligation or switching cost that genuinely exists now. Then choose stop, continue, change, or gather one missing fact.

Check-in: If I were deciding from today forward, what future cost and benefit would justify continuing, and what alternative am I giving up?

Evidence: Practical guidance; no evidence-like claim is detected in the current source record.

This protocol is for a narrow resource decision: you are considering whether to continue something, and past investment is dominating the argument. Separate history from the costs, benefits, obligations, and alternatives that still exist from today forward. It does not assume that stopping is automatically the better choice.

1. Name the continuation decision

Write the choice in present tense: continue, stop, pause, or change this commitment. Avoid starting with a defense of what you already spent.

2. Separate what cannot be recovered

List past time, money, or effort that will remain spent either way. Keep it for learning and accountability, but do not count it as a future benefit of continuing.

3. Compare the future paths

For each realistic option, note future cost, expected benefit, obligations, switching costs, and the best alternative use of the same resources. Use ranges or uncertainty labels when exact values are not justified.

4. Choose the next step

Stop, continue, change the commitment, or gather one missing fact. Record which current trade-off supports that choice. A specialist Cognitive Biases reference can explain the sunk-cost concept separately after that handoff has passed review.

Questions to consider

Does this mean I should quit whenever progress is slow?

No. Slow progress, switching costs, obligations, learning value, and future upside may all be valid reasons to continue. The protocol only separates unrecoverable past investment from reasons that still matter now.

What if I cannot estimate the future precisely?

Use ranges or qualitative comparisons and keep uncertainty visible. A rough comparison of feasible future paths is better than inventing precise probabilities or thresholds you cannot justify.
