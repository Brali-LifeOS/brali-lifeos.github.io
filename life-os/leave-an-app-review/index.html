Leave Useful Feedback

Turn a reaction into a concrete, actionable review

Protocol summary · practical

Try this

Describe one concrete thing that worked or failed, explain the effect it had on your task, and add one specific suggestion if you have one.

Check-in: Could the recipient understand what happened and what might improve without guessing what you meant?

Evidence: Practical guidance; no evidence-like claim is detected in the current source record.

Review note: Public content is replaced by a neutral observation-impact-suggestion feedback protocol. App-growth, engagement and outcome claims from inherited copy are not retained.

Useful feedback gives another person something they can inspect or act on. It is more informative than a vague score, compliment, or complaint.

1. State the observation

Describe what happened with enough detail that someone else could recognize the situation.

2. Explain the impact

Say what the event changed for you: time, clarity, confidence, completion, enjoyment, or another practical outcome.

3. Separate preference from defect

When something is mainly a preference, label it that way. This makes the feedback fairer and more useful.

4. Offer one suggestion

If you have a realistic improvement idea, add it briefly. Avoid turning the review into a full redesign brief unless that is actually requested.

Questions to consider

Should a review always include a suggestion?

No. A clear description of what happened can be enough. Add a suggestion only when you can make it specific and realistic.

How do I avoid an overly emotional review?

Write the concrete event first, then explain the effect on your task. You can still state a strong opinion without turning the whole review into a label about the product or people.

Try it, then review.

Use the action above as a starting point. Adapt it to your situation, notice what happens, and keep, change, or drop it.
