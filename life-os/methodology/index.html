How Brali thinks

Try the move. Understand the mechanism. Know where the evidence stops.

Brali is a library of small protocols, not a shrine to productivity tricks. A useful page should tell you what to try, why the move is plausible, what signal to watch, and what would make you change or stop it.

1 · Do

Start with an action

If a page needs three paragraphs of terminology before you know what to do, the page has work to do. Brali starts with the smallest useful move.

2 · Understand

Learn the mechanism

The goal is not to borrow scientific vocabulary. It is to understand what the protocol is changing: attention, timing, friction, feedback, retrieval, environment, or another observable part of the situation.

3 · Observe

Watch a real signal

Track the outcome the protocol is actually meant to change. A streak is not automatically progress, and a timer is not a biological law just because it has a reassuring countdown.

4 · Bound

Keep the claim smaller than the hype

If a study supports recall, Brali should not quietly upgrade that to intelligence. If it supports an acute response, it does not become a longevity protocol on the walk from the abstract to the headline.

Why the trust labels exist

Search visibility and recommendation trust are deliberately separate. Brali keeps the historical library discoverable so old material can be audited and improved, but people and AI agents should not receive an unreviewed page as if it had already earned the same confidence as a checked protocol.

That is why a page can exist publicly and still be review-gated. "We have a URL" is not an evidence state.

What source review changes

Research rarely arrives as a neat commandment. A review may support one outcome but not another; a trial may work in one population, for one duration, with several bundled components. Brali records those boundaries because they change the protocol. The interesting question is not simply did the paper have a positive result? It is what action does this result actually justify?

When evidence is mixed, Brali can keep a bounded experiment, narrow an existing page, mark a source as a challenge, or decline to create a new hack at all. Finding a paper is the beginning of editorial work, not the ceremonial end of it.

Claim debt is a review queue, not a verdict that a statement is false. Enforced categories block normal trusted recommendation when review is incomplete; monitor-only categories remain visible so editors can inspect broad effect, mechanism, and research-language wording without pretending a regular expression can replace source review.

How an entry earns trusted recommendation

  1. Start with a concrete action or protocol.
  2. Detect evidence-like wording, quantitative claims, effect estimates, high-confidence causal assertions, first-party results, guarantees, clinical-outcome wording, and sensitive topics.
  3. Record usable external sources when factual support is required.
  4. Review exact wording and sources rather than treating a recorded link as proof.
  5. Rewrite the action so the mechanism and evidence boundary are understandable to a non-specialist.
  6. Keep every public entry discoverable, but include only reviewed and practical entries in the Trusted Protocol Feed and normal AI recommendations.

Inherited corpus corrections

The migrated corpus is preserved for provenance. When the same inherited claim appears across many entries, Brali can apply a reviewed editorial normalization before public pages and evidence states are generated. 72 reviewed normalization rules are currently registered, affecting 9 source entries in this build.

These corrections are published with their rationale, review date, reviewer, source, and application count rather than silently rewriting the historical corpus.

Health and mental health

Brali's Growth Library is general educational material and a starting point for personal reflection. It is not medical diagnosis or treatment guidance. Sensitive entries receive a higher review bar and stay outside normal trusted recommendations until reviewed.

Machine-readable transparency

  • Trusted Protocol Feed — reviewed and low-risk practical protocols.
  • Evidence status index — evidence state for the complete library.
  • Review queue — entries still awaiting editorial work.
  • Claim debt report — claim categories, enforcement state, Topic aggregation, debt reasons, source presence, and review metadata.
  • Indexing policy output — complete search visibility and the separate trusted-recommendation set.
  • Editorial normalization register — reviewed inherited-claim corrections and where they were applied.

The static build fails if a public hack is missing from the sitemap, still carries noindex, loses its evidence state, or leaks a review-gated entry into the Trusted Protocol Feed. Quality gates are deliberately annoying in the useful way: they stop a nicer sentence from quietly becoming a worse claim.

Use Brali like a small experiment, not a belief system.

Choose a relevant protocol, check its evidence state, try the smallest useful version in your context, observe the intended signal, and keep, change, or stop the practice based on what happens and what the evidence actually supports.
