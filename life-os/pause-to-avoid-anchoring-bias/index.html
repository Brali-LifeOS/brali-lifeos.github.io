Pause Before Accepting the First Number or Frame

A short decision check for first-impression anchors

This protocol is for a simple decision problem: the first number, option, estimate, or framing is doing too much of the work. Brali keeps the intervention practical: name that starting point, create an independent alternative, and decide from current information. Use the linked Cognitive Biases reference, when available after review, for mechanism and evidence context.

1. Name the first anchor

Write the first number, option, estimate, or framing that entered the decision. Keep it separate from your conclusion. The goal is to expose the starting point, not to prove that it is wrong.

2. Generate an independent alternative

Produce at least one alternative from a different starting point: your own estimate, another feasible option, a comparable case, or a range based on current constraints.

3. Ask what information would change the choice

Identify one fact that could materially change the decision. If it is cheap and relevant to obtain, check it. If it is not, keep the uncertainty visible rather than replacing it with a precise-looking guess.

4. Decide from current information

Choose the next action and record the initial anchor, the alternative considered, and the fact or trade-off that drove the final choice. This page owns the workflow, not a claim that the workflow removes anchoring.

Questions to consider

Does this prove that the first option is wrong?

No. The first option may still be the best one. The purpose of the check is to make the starting point visible and compare it with at least one independent alternative before committing.

How much research should I do?

Only enough to obtain information that could materially change the choice. For a small reversible decision, one independent estimate or option may be enough; higher-stakes decisions may justify more checking.
