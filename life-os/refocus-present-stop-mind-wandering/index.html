Deliberate Attention Switching with Everyday Sounds

Practice selecting, switching, and broadening external attention

Protocol summary · reviewed

Try this

Find a safe place with several ordinary sounds. Attend closely to one sound without needing to block the others. Deliberately switch to a second and then a third sound. Finish by broadening attention so several sounds can be noticed together. Keep the exercise brief and stop when you choose.

Check-in: Could you intentionally select, switch, and broaden attention? Record the skill you practiced, not whether worry or another symptom changed.

Review note: Reviewed against 2016 and 2026 systematic-review literature on Attention Training Technique plus a 2021 randomized experimental study reporting no ATT advantage over control on worry outcomes in probable GAD. Public content is limited to the directly described attention operations: external selection, deliberate switching, and broadening/divided attention. It makes no treatment or symptom-reduction promise.

Safety: This is general educational material, not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, or a substitute for professional care.

Attention Training Technique research describes deliberate practice in externally focused selective attention, switching attention, and dividing or broadening attention. Brali extracts those observable attention operations into a small educational drill while keeping the clinical claims outside the protocol.

1. Choose several neutral sounds

Use ordinary sounds in a safe environment: ventilation, distant traffic, birds, footsteps, a clock, or other neutral background sounds. You do not need special audio or headphones.

2. Select one sound

Direct attention toward one sound and notice its changes. The goal is not to erase other sounds but to practice choosing the current target.

3. Switch deliberately

Choose a different sound and move attention to it on purpose. Repeat with another sound. Notice the act of switching rather than judging whether concentration feels perfect.

4. Broaden attention

Finish by allowing several sounds into awareness together. This changes the task from selecting one target to holding a wider external field.

Evidence boundary

ATT has been studied as a metacognitive intervention, but evidence varies by sample, outcome, protocol fidelity, and study quality. A recent systematic review found a mixed-quality evidence base, and a randomized study of probable generalized anxiety disorder did not find ATT superior to control for worry outcomes. This simplified Brali drill therefore claims only to practice attention selection and switching.

Questions to consider

Is this the standard Attention Training Technique or Metacognitive Therapy?

No. It is a simplified educational drill based on the attention operations described in ATT research. Formal ATT has a defined protocol, and Metacognitive Therapy is a broader treatment.

Will this reduce worry or anxiety?

Brali does not make that promise. Research on ATT is mixed across outcomes and study designs; one randomized study in people with probable generalized anxiety disorder found no advantage over the control condition for worry measures.

What should I measure?

Measure the controllable skill: whether you could deliberately select one sound, switch attention when intended, and broaden attention afterward. Do not treat a short-term symptom change as proof of treatment effect.
