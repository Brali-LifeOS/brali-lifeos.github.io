Start with trusted protocols

These entries currently meet Brali's reviewed/practical retrieval bar. “Trusted” is a publication state, not a promise that every protocol has equal scientific support.

  • Do Active Listening ExercisesIn one conversation, wait until the other person finishes a thought, paraphrase the main point in your own words, invite correction, and ask one open question before offering advice or changing the topic.practical
  • Make a Specific Repair Attempt After a Workable ConflictWait until you can speak without escalating. Name the specific action or words you are taking responsibility for. Acknowledge the impact as you understand it without using your intention to cancel the other person's experience. Apologize for your part without an 'if'. Offer one concrete repair or behavior change you can actually make, then ask how the other person sees it. Listen to the answer. Do not require forgiveness, agreement, or immediate trust in return.reviewed
  • Turn One Routine Exchange into a Genuine Micro-ConversationPick an ordinary low-pressure interaction that already needs to happen: buying coffee, greeting a neighbor, talking to a receptionist, or another brief exchange. If the context is appropriate, make eye contact, greet the person, and add one natural sentence or question. Keep it short and responsive to the other person's cues. If they are busy, uncomfortable, or not interested, let the interaction stay efficient.reviewed
