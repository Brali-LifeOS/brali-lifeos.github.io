  • Practice Accepting Uncertainty by Reminding Yourself That Not Everything Needs a Definite Answer or (Metacognitive)Practice accepting uncertainty by reminding yourself that not everything needs a definite answer or outcome. Let go of the urge to overthink.
  • Notice If You Believe Certain Thoughts Are 'true' or 'important' Just Because They Come (Metacognitive)Notice if you believe certain thoughts are 'true' or 'important' just because they come up. Ask yourself if these beliefs are helping you or holding you back.
  • When a Negative Thought Arises, Take a Step Back Mentally and Observe It as (Metacognitive)When a negative thought arises, take a step back mentally and observe it as if you’re watching it from a distance. Notice it without engaging.
  • Deliberate Attention Switching with Everyday SoundsUse several neutral sounds around you to practice deliberately selecting one sound, switching to another, and then noticing several at once. Treat this as an attention-flexibility
  • Set a Specific Time Each Day for Rumination (e (Metacognitive)Set a specific time each day for rumination (e.g., 10 minutes). If you catch yourself ruminating outside of this time, gently remind yourself to wait until your ‘thinking time.’
  • When a Thought Arises, Remind Yourself That It’s Just a Mental Event, Not Necessarily (Metacognitive)When a thought arises, remind yourself that it’s just a mental event, not necessarily a reflection of reality. Thoughts are often just ideas or possibilities.
  • Observe Recurring Thought Patterns, Like Worry or Self-Doubt (Metacognitive)Observe recurring thought patterns, like worry or self-doubt. Simply recognizing these patterns helps you detach from them over time.
