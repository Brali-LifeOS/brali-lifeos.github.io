Stress Regulation

Use low-risk practices to respond to everyday stress and regain workable perspective.

Legacy collections: Be Calm.

Status: active. 9 current library entries map here through legacy or reviewed record-level classification.

  • Spend 3–5 Minutes Focusing on Your SensesSpend 3–5 minutes focusing on your senses. Notice what you see, hear, smell, feel, and taste. Gently redirect your attention whenever your mind wanders.
  • Comfortable Breathing ResetUse a few comfortable, unforced breaths as a brief pause before deciding what to do next. Skip counts that create strain or dizziness.
  • Comfortable Diaphragmatic BreathingTry a few slow, comfortable breaths while noticing the abdomen move. Keep the exercise easy, stop if it feels uncomfortable, and do not use it instead of medical care.
  • Short Resilience ReminderCreate a short phrase that acknowledges difficulty without pretending it is easy, then points back to one action you can still control.
  • Draw Two Circles; One for Factors You Can Control and Another for Those YouDraw two circles; one for factors you can control and another for those you cannot. Set clear, achievable goals based on what’s within your control.
  • Pause Before Acting on a High-Stress UrgeWhen a situation is safe enough to pause, notice the urge to react, describe what is happening, choose a harmless short delay, and then reassess the next action. This is not full D
  • Practice Deep Breathing Exercises for a Few Minutes Each DayPractice deep breathing exercises for a few minutes each day.
  • Make a Point to Engage in Social Activities Regularly, Whether It's a Virtual Meetup,Make a point to engage in social activities regularly, whether it's a virtual meetup, a coffee with a friend, or joining a club or group.
  • When Faced with Difficulties, Breathe Deeply and Accept the Situation as It Is, WithoutWhen faced with difficulties, breathe deeply and accept the situation as it is, without resistance.
