Where partnerships can create value

  • Commercial dataset licensing: selected or complete Brali collections for paid products and services.
  • Hosted data/API access: a future managed search and retrieval layer for teams that do not want to maintain static datasets.
  • MCP and agent integrations: search, retrieve, recommend, and cite Brali protocols from AI workflows.
  • Custom collections: curated protocol sets for learning, workplace effectiveness, wellbeing, creativity, decision-making, or other defined domains.
  • Localization: translation plus terminology, cultural adaptation, evidence review, and language-specific discovery.
  • Research collaboration: taxonomy, evidence methods, recommendation quality, personal experimentation, and evaluation.
  • Editorial partnerships: source review and domain-expert validation for high-impact collections.
  • Embedded experiences: protocol cards, recommendation widgets, guided experiments, or white-label knowledge components.

A commercial model that does not lock up the public good

The public library can remain readable and useful for individuals, researchers, and non-commercial projects while commercial use funds the expensive parts: curation, evidence review, localization, maintenance, integration, and service guarantees.

  1. Community and research: public non-commercial access under the repository license.
  2. Commercial data license: permission to embed and redistribute defined Brali datasets in revenue-generating products.
  3. Hosted integration: future API or MCP access with search, filtering, versioning, and operational support.
  4. Custom work: domain collections, localization, evidence enrichment, or product-specific integration.

What a partner receives

The important asset is not a bag of inspirational sentences. It is a maintainable knowledge graph-like hierarchy: Life Area → Growth Zone → protocol → action/check-in → evidence state → canonical source. The public feeds make the contract inspectable before a commercial discussion starts.

Principles

  • One canonical source of truth for human pages and machine interfaces.
  • Evidence status remains visible downstream.
  • Stable identifiers and URLs matter more than fashionable front ends.
  • Translations preserve identity and provenance.
  • Commercial agreements do not grant rights to the Brali name or logo unless explicitly stated.
  • Sensitive guidance keeps a higher editorial and safety bar.
