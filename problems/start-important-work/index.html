Start important work when you keep putting it off

How do I start important work when I keep procrastinating?

Separate a vague task, start-friction, and attention-switching problem before choosing a protocol.

How to use this page: start with the decision path, then choose one protocol that fits the actual bottleneck. Recommendations come only from Brali protocols already classified as reviewed or practical. Decision guidance is editorial synthesis, not a claim that one sequence is universally best.

Choose the bottleneck first

Decision 1

The task is still vague

Define one visible next action before adding timers, blockers, or motivation tricks.

Decision 2

The next action is clear but starting still feels heavy

Shrink the commitment to a deliberately small first block. The goal is to cross the start line, not finish the whole project.

Decision 3

You start but quickly switch away

Protect one short focus block and remove one predictable interruption instead of redesigning your entire productivity system.

Stop rule: If progress depends on missing information, approval, access, or another person, treat that dependency as the next action rather than diagnosing yourself with a motivation problem.

Trusted protocols that fit this problem

Flagship 100 · reviewed

Deliberate Attention Switching with Everyday Sounds

Find a safe place with several ordinary sounds. Attend closely to one sound without needing to block the others. Deliberately switch to a second and then a third sound. Finish by broadening attention so several sounds can be noticed together. Keep the exercise brief and stop when you choose.

Check-in: Could you intentionally select, switch, and broaden attention? Record the skill you practiced, not whether worry or another symptom changed.

Flagship 100 · practical

Alternate Focused Work With Real Breaks

Choose one important task, protect a focus block from avoidable interruptions, then step away for a real break. Adjust both periods to your workload and energy rather than forcing a fixed 90/30 schedule.

Check-in: Was the focus block long enough to make progress, did the break actually feel like a break, and what duration would fit the next cycle better?

Flagship 100 · practical

Use a Short Phrase to Choose the Next Step

When you feel stuck, pause and repeat a short phrase once. Name what is difficult in plain language, then choose one action that is small enough to begin now. Skip the phrase if it feels hollow, pressuring or unrelated to the real problem.

Check-in: What was difficult, what action did I choose, and did the phrase help me begin or should I use a different cue next time?

Evidence boundaries

support-existing · reviewed boundary

Give me a break! A systematic review and meta-analysis on the efficacy of micro-breaks for increasing well-being and performance

Across the included studies, micro-breaks of up to 10 minutes were associated on average with small improvements in vigor and reduced fatigue. The pooled overall performance effect was not statistically significant, and performance effects varied by task demands and break duration. This supports retaining a short, adjustable break as a bounded recovery practice without presenting it as a reliable performance booster.

Does not establish: A 5-minute break is an evidence-based optimal duration. Twenty-five minutes of work followed by five minutes of rest is superior to other focus schedules.

challenge-existing · reviewed boundary

Targeting Procrastination Using Psychological Treatments: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Across the reviewed randomized comparisons, psychological treatments targeting procrastination had a small average post-treatment benefit on self-reported procrastination relative to inactive controls, but effects varied substantially between studies and the evidence base had important quality limitations. This supports treating task initiation as a legitimate intervention target, not claiming that one brief Brali routine or one component has been validated.

Does not establish: A five-minute start is an evidence-based or optimal duration for overcoming procrastination. Completing one visible first action by itself reduces procrastination, improves productivity or reliably creates momentum.
