Living research pipeline

Brali keeps the research process as data instead of hiding it inside one prompt.

  1. Search lenses: research-queries.json maps maintained scholarly queries to Domains, Topics, Methods, and Lenses.
  2. Discovery queue: the weekly Research Scout deduplicates recent scholarly metadata into research-candidates.json.
  3. Evidence decision: an Evidence Reviewer reads the actual source and records the narrow claim it supports, challenges, or fails to support in evidence-decisions.json.
  4. Library change: only then can a candidate strengthen an existing hack, propose a new atomic hack, or become part of an executable protocol.

Why the queue is deliberately untrusted

Search metadata is useful for discovery, not for conclusions. A title can sound relevant while the population, intervention, outcome, effect size, or limitations say something much narrower. Candidate records therefore cannot receive Brali's reviewed evidence state without an explicit source review and evidence decision.

What belongs here

  • Evidence notes: check an idea against research and explain what the sources do and do not support.
  • Trend notes: watch useful developments in behavior change, learning, wellbeing, productivity, and AI-assisted tools.
  • Open questions: record questions that could improve the library, recommendations, evidence model, protocols, or translations.
  • Contradictions: record good evidence that challenges a popular hack instead of quietly ignoring it.

From research to the library

A research note or candidate does not automatically become a trusted protocol. It can improve an existing hack, add or remove a claim, change an evidence state after review, create a new atomic hack proposal, suggest a protocol composed from existing actions, become a challenge-existing correction that narrows a claim, or remain a watch decision when the evidence is informative but not yet actionable.
