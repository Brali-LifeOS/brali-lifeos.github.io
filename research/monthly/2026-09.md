- **Nutrition if-then planning** — proposed narrowly for adults who already intend to increase fruit and vegetable intake; no medical or optimal-diet claims.

### 2 September

- **Personal-finance goal setting** — kept in screening because the complete article had not been reliably reviewed. Abstract-level results are not enough for Brali evidence.

### 3 September

- **Leisure crafting** — proposed as a bounded personal-development experiment around autonomy, learning and connection, without turning leisure into a productivity obligation.
- **Supervisory feedback** — kept on watch. The synthesis is useful for design hypotheses, but the underlying literature is largely correlational and highly heterogeneous.

### 4 September

- **Records-maintenance block** — proposed from a large preregistered field experiment, but only where retention and archival rules are already known. No indiscriminate deletion and no claim that a calendar reminder alone caused the effect.

### 5 September

- **Break up prolonged sitting** — existing protocol strengthened, but cognitive claims were narrowed: small acute executive-function and memory effects are possible; attention and productivity should not be promised.

## Fresh September publication scan

These papers were published during 2–4 September and were screened separately from the existing Brali queue.

### Hold: self-guided resilience intervention

A 2 September randomized trial of 216 university students found some favourable per-protocol results, but adherence was low and the intention-to-treat analysis found no significant effects. This is useful boundary evidence against casually promoting a generic self-guided resilience course from completer-only effects.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-67088-7

### Challenge: growth-mindset feedback as a default

A 2 September controlled study of 79 German secondary-school students compared performance-based feedback with growth-mindset feedback in programming education. It found no main effect of feedback type on performance, mindset beliefs, self-efficacy, attempts or giving up. The sample is narrow, but the result is enough to challenge the assumption that growth-mindset wording is automatically a better feedback design.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10758-026-09996-1

### Watch: human–AI decision revision

A 2 September Scientific Reports study analysed 549 UK professionals and examined how individual traits related to whether people revised a budget allocation after AI input. It is relevant to Brali's human–AI calibration lens, but it is observational and cannot justify a causal delegation or decision-calibration protocol.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-69355-z

### Reject for now: app-based exercise for academicians

A 4 September quasi-experimental study compared app-guided exercise, traditional exercise and control among 96 academicians. The intervention is interesting, but allocation was not randomized and exercise was bundled with ergonomic education. Brali already has stronger movement evidence, so this does not justify another protocol.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-68695-0

## What changed in the project this month

Beyond evidence work, September also moved Brali's distribution layer forward:

- AI discovery surfaces were opened.
- A Google Search indexing loop was added and its runner corrected.
- Research lenses were expanded for pretesting, personal finance and leisure crafting.
- Research records were corrected when a new source duplicated an existing candidate or when workflow status drifted from the Evidence Decision.
- A monthly digest generator was added so the repository can produce the same audit surface every month from canonical research registries and Git history.

## Monthly rule from now on

The monthly loop should answer five questions:

1. What new research or practical signals appeared during the calendar month?
2. What did Brali actually review rather than merely discover?
3. What was added or used to strengthen an existing protocol?
4. What was rejected, challenged or left on watch — and why?
5. What changed in the project itself, and what gaps should drive the next month?

Negative and null findings count as useful output. A month in which Brali rejects five weak ideas can be more valuable than a month in which it adds five near-duplicate hacks.

This file is an audit digest, not an evidence source. Canonical promotion still requires an explicit Evidence Decision.
